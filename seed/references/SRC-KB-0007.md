The video explores how to deploy AI agents for business automation and monetize them through equity-based partnerships rather than recurring monthly fees. Corey Ganim demonstrates real agents he built for his own business, including a sales inquiry triage system using Hermes agents with persistent memory and integration with GitHub. He contrasts GrokBot (for disposable, single-task bots) with Hermes (for long-term, self-improving agents). The video outlines a business model where operators install agent-driven growth systems for clients and earn equity when those systems generate significant revenue growth.

# Core method

Build an AI agent for your own business to validate its utility, then deploy it as a managed agent or equity partnership for clients. Use tools like Orgo to manage agent skills and cron jobs without exposing client-facing tools. Structure the partnership around equity ownership tied to the client’s revenue growth, not monthly fees. Prioritize agents with persistent memory and integration with business systems (like CRM or knowledge bases) to maximize long-term value.

# Key takeaways

* Use Hermes agents for long-term, self-improving automation with persistent memory, while GrokBot is better for disposable, single-task bots.
* Deploy agents via Orgo as an internal tool for your team, keeping client-facing tools abstracted (e.g., clients never see Orgo).
* Monetize agents through equity partnerships where you earn a percentage of the client’s profit growth (e.g., 4x multiple on $5K/month fee).
* Build agents that integrate with business systems (e.g., GitHub for knowledge, CRM for sales) to ensure they deliver sustained value.
* Target businesses with missing systems (e.g., CRM, Google Ads) as a niche — it’s often more valuable than targeting a specific industry.
* Use a ‘done for you’ model: spin up agents, turn them into skills, deploy, and manage them for clients (e.g., insurance claims processing).
